# Artificial intelligence

We are committed to ensuring all Australians share the benefits of artificial intelligence (AI).

## Australia’s plan for AI

The Australian Government’s ambition is to harness AI technologies to create a fairer, stronger Australia where every person shares the benefits.

AI is reshaping the global economy and how Australians work, learn and connect. It can:

- close service gaps in health, disability and aged care
- improve education and employment opportunities
- create secure, well-paid jobs in future industries
- create an environment for innovation.

To remain competitive, productive and resilient, we need coordinated action to seize the potential of AI while managing its risks.

The National AI Plan sets out the government’s direction on AI. It focusses on:

- capturing the opportunity
- spreading the benefits
- keeping Australians safe.

### [National AI Plan](https://web.archive.org/publications/national-ai-plan)

2 December 2025

A whole-of-government framework to ensure technology works for everyone. It will guide government, industry, research and communities to work with AI.

## Capturing the AI opportunity

We are capturing the opportunity for Australia to become a global leader in developing and adopting trusted, secure and responsible AI.

- ### [National AI Centre](https://web.archive.org/science-technology-and-innovation/technology/artificial-intelligence/national-ai-centre)

NAIC is the government’s lead body supporting industry to unlock the economic benefits of AI.
- ### [AI Accelerator initiative](https://web.archive.org/news/ai-accelerator-initiative-kicks-funding-industry-led-research)

Round 28 of the Cooperative Research Centres (CRC) Program will support AI development and commercialisation by businesses and researchers across Australia.
- ### [Setting expectations for data centres](https://web.archive.org/publications/expectations-data-centres-and-ai-infrastructure-developers)

The government’s expectations will help incentivise investment in data centres that align with Australia’s interest.
- ### [Collaborating with leading AI and technology companies](https://web.archive.org/science-technology-and-innovation/technology/artificial-intelligence/collaborating-ai-and-technology-companies)

The government is establishing arrangements with leading companies to strengthen our national AI capabilities as part of the National AI Plan.

## Spreading the benefits

Every Australian should be able to benefit from AI, regardless of age, location or gender. We are spreading the benefits of AI by supporting adoption. AI adoption has the potential to improve business productivity and deliver better wages, job satisfaction and stability for workers.

- ### [AI Adopt Program](https://www.ai.gov.au/about/connect-us/ai-adopt-centres)

The program funds AI adopt centres to help small and medium businesses use AI in a practical and responsible way.
- ### [Guidance for AI Adoption](https://ai.gov.au/staying-safe-and-responsible/essential-ai-practices/guidance-ai-adoption-implementation-guidance)

This practical resource from the National AI Centre helps Australian business adopt AI safely and responsibly. It evolves the Voluntary AI Safety Standard and Australia’s AI Ethics Principles.

## Keeping Australians safe

To protect Australians and maintain trust and confidence in AI systems, we’re committed to understanding and responding to advanced AI.

- ### [Australia's AI Safety Institute](https://web.archive.org/science-technology-and-innovation/technology/artificial-intelligence/ai-safety-institute)

The AI Safety Institute is monitoring, testing, and analysing advanced AI capabilities, risks, harms and trends to help the Australian Government keep Australians safe.

## Partnering internationally on responsible AI

[Read more](https://web.archive.org/publications?pub-search=&pub-topic=2964&pub-category=2986&publisher=All&field_date_value=All&items_per_page=10)

Australia engages internationally on AI to support Australian innovation and to shape international AI governance.

- ### [Paris AI Action Summit statement](https://web.archive.org/news/australia-signs-paris-ai-action-summit-statement)

The Statement on Inclusive and Sustainable Artificial Intelligence for People and the Planet builds on the work of the Bletchley and Seoul Declarations.
- ### [Global Partnership on Artificial Intelligence (GPAI)](https://web.archive.org/news/australia-supports-safe-ai-through-new-gpai-declaration)

Australia is collaborating on the responsible use and development of AI as a founding member of GPAI.
- ### [EU-Australia Digital Economy and Technology Policy Dialogue](https://web.archive.org/news/australia-and-european-union-deepen-digital-relationship)

Australia and the European Union are building on our existing relationship to strengthen ties on priority digital and technology issues.
- ### [Australia-Singapore Cyber and Digital Senior Officials Dialogue](https://web.archive.org/news/singapore-and-australia-strengthen-collaboration-ai-and-cyber-security)

Australian and Singapore are building on a memorandum of understanding to cooperate on AI.
- ### [Hiroshima AI Process Friends Group](https://web.archive.org/news/australia-joins-hiroshima-ai-process-friends-group)

Australia is part of the Hiroshima AI Process Friends Group. The group broadens the Hiroshima AI Process beyond G7 members.
- ### [Seoul Declaration](https://web.archive.org/publications/seoul-declaration-countries-attending-ai-seoul-summit-21-22-may-2024)

Australia and 9 other countries signed the declaration. It follows the Bletchley Declaration and confirms a shared understanding of the opportunities and risks posed by AI.
- ### [Bletchley Declaration on AI safety](https://web.archive.org/publications/bletchley-declaration-countries-attending-ai-safety-summit-1-2-november-2023)

Australia and 27 other countries signed the declaration at the AI Safety Summit in the United Kingdom.
